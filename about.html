The atelier

Composed, not assembled.

The Gilded Basket began with a simple irritation: gift baskets that looked like warehouse stock wearing a ribbon. We set up a small packing bench in Lahore and decided to treat every basket the way an editor treats a page — a point of view first, contents second.

How we work

A small bench, a short shelf, strong opinions

We keep the catalogue deliberately short — fourteen baskets — because a short shelf can be a graded shelf. Almonds are checked by hand. Chocolate is tasted by batch. Dates are bought in season and packed within the week, never warehoused through a summer.

Nothing is pre-packed. When an order arrives, a packer composes the basket against the recipe card, photographs it for the record, and seals it with ribbon and wax. The card is written by hand, in ink, by a person whose handwriting we chose on purpose.

It is slower than a warehouse. It is also the entire point.

What we hold to

Three rules the bench runs on

Graded, or not at all

If an ingredient would not be served at our own table, it does not enter a basket. Suppliers are small, named on our recipe cards, and changed when quality slips.

Packed to order

Pre-packed baskets age. Ours are composed after you order — which is why delivery takes 48 hours of packing care rather than 48 minutes of shelf dust.

Words by hand

A printed card says “a gift was purchased”. A hand-written card says someone meant it. Every basket carries the second kind, at no charge.

See the standard

Judge us by the shelf.

Fourteen baskets, every contents list published in full. Start with the bestsellers — or compose your own and hold us to your standard instead.
